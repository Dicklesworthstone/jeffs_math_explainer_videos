| Topic (owner's words) | "Use the fishing exercise from Feller book 1 … estimate the total number of fish. Why does this work? How exactly? What's the intuition?" |
| Working title | **How to Count Fish You've Never Seen** (alternative: *How Many Fish Are in the Lake?*) |
| One-sentence takeaway | Two independent catches form a little table (fish caught twice, caught only first, caught only second, and a dark cell nobody caught); the hypergeometric distribution shows that the best guess for the dark cell is the one that balances the table, which is exactly the proportion guess N ≈ M·n/k, and it says honestly how far off that guess might be. |
| The hook (where the obvious tool fails) | "Dip a net and multiply by the lake" gives answers tens of times apart, because fish crowd into weeds and deep water and avoid the open; a net cannot tell you how much of the lake it searched. The marked fish fix exactly this: they measure the net. |
| Cold open (≤ 20 s, plan rule 20) | The lake reorganises into the table **100 \| 900 / 900 \| ?**; the dark cell fills with "≈ 8,100"; then the title; then the honest failure of the one-patch estimate (plan §9.4, 01_1–01_6). |
| The one picture | **The four-cell table** on dark water. Feller's likelihood ratio reduces to Mn − kN = ab − k·u, so the best dark cell is the one that balances the diagonals: 900 · 900 = 100 · 8,100. The mixing picture (fish density stays clumpy while the marked fraction goes flat) is why the table is legitimate. |
| Worked examples | (1) a 20-fish toy lake (M = 5, nets of 4): all 4,845 netfuls countable; (2) a likelihood toy (M = 10, n = 10, k = 3): best lake size 33; (3) Feller's lake (M = n = 1,000, k = 100): 8,100 unseen, 10,000 in all. |
| Feller's source | *An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications*, Vol. I, 3rd ed. (1968), Ch. II §6, Example (c), pp. 45–46 (1st ed. 1950: §II.5 Example (e), pp. 37–38). Feller's lake is the tie case: 9,999 and 10,000 are both maximum-likelihood sizes, and the video says so. His "8,500 to 12,000" is about a 93.6% interval; the exact 95% interval is [8,400, 12,073]. On-screen quotes name their edition. |
