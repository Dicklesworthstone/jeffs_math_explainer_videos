## Comprehension check

| Chapter | What I would tell a friend |
|---|---|
| 01_the_book | Two quarter turns of a book in the two orders leave it facing different ways, while two slides land on the same spot, so a gripper's pose cannot be handled by adding numbers. |
| 02_one_turn | The two quarter turns together are one turn of 120° about an axis neither used; that turns close up like this (Euler) and can be undone makes them a group, and the computing is done by multiplying 3 by 3 matrices, later turn on the left. |
| 03_three_angles | Three nested rings name an orientation with three angles, but at a quarter turn of the middle ring two axes coincide and one way of turning is gone; Apollo lived with it, and no three-number scheme escapes some defect, for a topological reason. |
| 04_halfway | Averaging the numbers 170 and −170 gives the heading opposite to the true midpoint; taking the difference as a turn, halving it on the line that touches the circle and putting it back is the pattern the rest of the video reuses. |
| 05_spin | The flat piece at "doing nothing" is the space of angular velocities; holding one for a second is the exponential, Rodrigues' formula computes it, and the pair (group, flat piece) is a Lie group and its Lie algebra. |
| 06_the_base | On a floor a constant velocity in the base's own frame integrates to a turn about a fixed centre, not to velocity times time: 1.91 m to the left, facing back, not 3 m ahead. |
| 07_screws | In space a velocity is a twist of six numbers, a constant twist is a screw motion, and every change of pose is a single screw (Mozzi, Chasles); a pose has six degrees of freedom although global descriptions carry more numbers. |
| 08_seven_screws | Each hinge is a screw with no slide acting on everything beyond it, so the gripper's pose is seven exponentials times the home pose, the right-hand factor acting first. |
| 09_by_hand | The formula is run for shoulder and elbow at a quarter turn each (tip at (0.42, 0, 0.886) m) and then joint one; the screws are the axes as they stood at zero, never where they are now. |
| 10_velocities_add | The gripper's twist is linear in the joint speeds; the Jacobian's columns are the joints' screws at the present pose, and a joint's effect on the tip is its lever, the tip's distance from that joint's axis. |
| 11_lost_directions | Where the Jacobian loses rank the gripper loses directions (4 of 6 fully stretched), and near such a pose a slow pull demands an elbow speed that grows like 1 over the sine of the bend; the wrist's three axes are the gimbal again. |
| 12_the_spare_joint | Seven joints against six leaves a one-dimensional set of joint speeds that move nothing: the elbow circles the shoulder to wrist line at radius 0.29 m with the gripper fixed. |
| 13_steps | Inverse kinematics as Newton's method on the group: the error is the twist joining the pose held to the pose wanted, the Jacobian turns it into a joint step, and this run needs three steps. |
| 14_the_straight_path | Averaging two rotation matrices entry by entry leaves the group (determinant cos²(a/2)); scaling the joining twist gives a path of true poses, and planners often split turn and slide instead, 7.6 cm apart here. |
| 15_everywhere | The same arithmetic runs floating bodies and integrators (adding a spin inflates the determinant by 1 + dt² a step), and the mismatch between the two orders of two small turns is a turn of about the product of the angles about the third axis: the Lie bracket. |
