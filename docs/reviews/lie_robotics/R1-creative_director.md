# R1 (storyboard) review: lie_robotics, creative director

Reviewer: creative_director. Stage: R1, the storyboard. Written without reading the other reviewers' files.

## What I checked, and how

Read in full: `tools/review_prompts/creative_director.md`; `docs/reviews/lie_robotics/R1-packet.md` (brief, storyboard, fact sheet, every fact table); plan §5.1 and §6.1 to §6.4, plus §7.5's notes on listen windows; `tools/storyboard.py` (the length rule and the showcase check); `explainer_corpus/lie_robotics/src/score.rs` (the identity).

Recomputed with my own scripts, outside the repository (NumPy only, none of the repo's code):

- **Length.** The gate's rule (words at 2.68 a second, 0.6 s a beat, 2 s a prediction, 2.5 s a hold, listen N s) gives 965.8 s = 16.1 min, which matches the brief. Every cost below is in that currency. The chapter headers' "~N s" sum to 1,040 s (17.3 min).
- **The arm.** A product-of-exponentials model built from `G-A.axis.*` and the axis heights: tip at zero (0, 0, 1.306), rank 3; the L shape (0.420, 0, 0.886), rank 6; stretched (0.946, 0, 0.360), rank 4, residual of the slide along the arm 0.9409. All match the fact sheet. The same model says which directions are lost when stretched (finding C6).
- **Chapter 14's screw.** Radius 0.2589 m about (0.355, 0.045); bow r(1 − cos 45°) = 0.0758 m. Matches.
- **The base.** Spin 0, 30 and 60 degrees a second for 6 s at 0.5 m/s ends at (3.00, 0), (0, 1.91), (0, 0).
- **The leftover turn.** 120°, 1.7409° and 0.0175° at 90°, 10° and 1°, matching `G-B.leftover.*`; its axis is 54.7°, 39.2°, 20.8°, 7.1° and 0.7° from vertical at 90°, 60°, 30°, 10° and 1°.
- **The swelling.** (1 + 10⁻⁴)¹⁰⁰⁰ = 1.1052 in volume is 5.1% in width, across the spin's axis only.
- **The blend.** Two turns a quarter turn apart blend to a scale of 0.7071 across the axis; 0.7071² = 0.50.
- **Colour.** ΔE2000 between the nine role colours, with the same Lab conversion and formula as `fair_coin`'s test (the Sharma, Wu and Dalal pair gives 2.0425).

Not checked: every other number in the fact sheet (unchecked, not "fine"); the sources; engine feasibility of anything I propose. Nothing has been rendered: this video's `src/chapters/` holds animatic stubs only and no `draw.rs`, so every judgement about legibility is made from the storyboard's text. There is no cue sheet, so "does a cue fight the voice" is unchecked.

None of the fixes below needs a moving camera. C3 is a flat affine morph of one group.

## The director's questions, in brief

- **Do one picture and one move carry fifteen chapters?** Not yet. A gold axis or thread is on screen in 11 of 15 chapters (absent in 03, 04 and 11; in 05 only an orange arrow), but it is drawn as a new object each time, has no specification, and is born by a cut (C3, C10, C22). The move is spoken in nine chapters and drawn in two (04, and 05_7 as a card); where it could be drawn it is drawn as its opposite (C4) or as a flow chart (C1). Chapters 10 to 12 read as a contents page because no question is open (C2).
- **Is the cold open a hook, and is the payoff previewed early enough?** The puzzle is a real one. As timed it is 24 s of instructions, the title lands at 42 s and the robot of the title at 46 s (C5). The preview at 56 to 68 s is early enough, but its words promise only the line, which is paid at 8:42 of 16:06 (C2).
- **A "huh" in every chapter?** Yes. The thinnest is 08, whose surprise is the line itself.
- **Do the quizzes make the viewer feel smart?** Nine do (01_1, 03_2, 04_2, 05_4, 06_2, 08_3, 10_4, 12_2, 14_2). Two are a guess and a surprise (02_2, 13_4), which is fine. 07_6 is recall (C14). 09_5 and 15_3 have nothing at stake (C17, C18). 11_2 is a yes or no that its own wording answers; it can stand once C6 fixes the picture that follows it.
- **Where is continuity broken?** Objects replaced: C3, C21, C22. A colour for two ideas: C8. Colours too close to tell apart: C7.
- **Weakest chapter:** 13. Single change: C1.
- **Is the ending the rigorous form of the opening?** In content, yes. In picture, no (C9).
- **Is any spectacle decoration?** 10_6's 42 running numbers (C16), 13_5's bars (C1), 06_1's code panel and 15_1's vignettes (C19). The opposite problem is larger: there is no set piece at all (C28) and no sound that teaches (C11).
- **Three frames to remember.** (1) 14_4 as redrawn in C10: the steel arm, its gripper on a gold thread with stair-tread spokes about a vertical gold axis, the pink goal on the table. This is the thumbnail. (2) 01_2: two books, the same two turns, covers facing two ways. (3) 04_3 into 04_4: the number line wrapped on the circle, the red seam, the orange line touching. Runner-up: 12_4, the elbow's circle over a readout of 0.000. Frames (1) and (2) together are nearly 15_6's last frame, which is as it should be.
