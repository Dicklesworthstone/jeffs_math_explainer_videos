## Comprehension check

| ch | The sentence I would say to a friend |
|---|---|
| 01 | Counting fish in one square and scaling up gives answers 31× apart (2,800 to 88,000 in the same lake), because fish clump and a net cannot know what share of the fish it reached; two marked catches instead give a table whose unseen cell, about 8,100, can be filled in with an error bar. |
| 02 | The second net caught 100 of the 1,000 marked fish, which measures the net's catch fraction (one in ten), so the lake is about 1,000 × 10 = 10,000; this is the cross-multiplication k/M ≈ n/N, backed by E[K] = nM/N exactly. |
| 03 | The guess does not need fish to be spread evenly (they stay clumped); it needs marked status to stop depending on position once the fish mix, so any net's marked fraction matches the lake's. |
| 04 | In a 20-fish lake, sorting all 4,845 equally likely netfuls by how many marked fish they hold gives the hypergeometric law, which gives the chances of every catch once the lake is known. |
| 05 | Fixing the observed catch and varying the lake size turns those chances into a score (the likelihood); it peaks at 33, and its total is 50/3, not 1, because the lake size is not random. |
| 06 | Feller's ratio says the score rises exactly while Mn − kN = ab − k·u > 0, so the best lake balances the table's diagonals (k·u = a·b), which is ⌊Mn/k⌋; at Feller's numbers that is the tie 9,999 / 10,000. |
| 07 | In a known lake of 10,000, Mn/k scatters lopsidedly and on average a little high (central 95% 8,475–12,048, exact mean 10,082.5), because dividing by k bends the spread; it breaks when k = 0, and Chapman's +1 version never divides by zero and is exactly unbiased when M + n ≥ N. |
| 08 | Turning the question around (which lake sizes make our catch unsurprising) gives [8,400, 12,073], a procedure that contains the true size at least 95% of the time whatever it is, and whose relative width shrinks like 1/√k. |
| 09 | **As drafted:** "the estimate fails when the catches stop being independent *or the lake changes*, except when a third of the fish die, which changes nothing." That is a rule plus its own counterexample, so the chapter's takeaway is **wrong as drafted (blocker, R-01)**. The correct sentence: every bias is the second catch failing to be a fair copy of the lake's marked fraction. |
| 10 | Laplace's France, two code reviewers and Kosovo's four lists all fill an unseen cell from the cells we can see, and each carries its own caveat: communes not chosen at random, bugs that differ in difficulty, log-linear models. |
