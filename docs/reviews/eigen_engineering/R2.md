# R2 dispositions — eigen_engineering (Video K)

Round R2 on the final script and fact sheet (plan §5.2, §5.3), 2026-10-13, after
the P3 cut (17.6 to 14.98 minutes by the G2 estimate). Reviews (verbatim):
`R2-maya_14.md` (M), `R2-dev_28.md` (D), `R2-ruth_45.md` (R, with the
mathematician audit) and `R2-engineer.md` (EN, a practising structural
engineer, acoustician and economist read together). 30 + 22 + 31 + 8 = 91
findings, plus 8 + 4 + 4 delights kept. The packet they read is regenerated by
`./x eigen_engineering review script` and is not committed.

Dispositions: **fixed** (in `explainer_corpus/eigen_engineering/docs/storyboard.md`,
`description.md` or the fact sheet; line ids are the storyboard's now),
**answered** (why not, one sentence) or **deferred** (to the owner or a bead).
No blocker was raised. Gate: no finding without a disposition.

**Method.** Four fresh readers, each with its own persona prompt, read the same
packet and none read another's review or R1. Their findings are proposals: a
finding that a computation or a source could settle was settled here before it
was accepted (the audit column below says which). The mathematician audit is
Ruth's checklist (`ruth_audit.md`) run item by item, and the engineer lens is
the second half of the order's question ("which an engineer would object to").
The `modes-of-reasoning-project-analysis` discipline was applied in its lean
form: one named question (which claims are false, mislead a tenth-grader or
would be objected to by a practitioner, so that a revision changes the words
before narration), independent readers, claims audited by someone other than
their author, checked nulls allowed (every reviewer returned several), and no
quotas; its run-directory ledger was not built, because `R2.md` is the record.

**Provenance of the review files.** `R2-dev_28.md` and `R2-ruth_45.md` were
saved by their own reviewers. For `R2-maya_14.md` and `R2-engineer.md` the
reviewers' own writes were cut off part-way by an API safety classifier (a 1.3
KB fragment and a header and a table's first row were left); the producer wrote
both files complete from the text the reviewers returned, with no edits except
one path in the engineer's file removed for the public-hygiene rule. Each file
says so at its top. Finding counts per file: Maya 30 + 8 delights, Dev 22 + 4,
Ruth 31 + 4, engineer 8. If a reader needs the reviewers' original wording
beyond these files, it exists nowhere else.

**What was verified by recomputation here** (the P3 claim-walk script, shared
with neither `facts.rs` nor `oracle.py`; 155 spoken numbers matched, 140
checks): the lone-load shares (75.8%, 41.8%, 86.3%; ten patterns for 2.5%
under a lone midspan load); that (30, 10) keeps 35% and (10, 30) keeps 25% of
what is made; the dot-product recipe giving (20, 5.38) and rebuilding
(36.2, 9.2), not (30, 10); the mode-to-pattern overlaps (1.00, 0.99, 0.98,
0.86, 0.85, 0.79, then 0.10 and 0.02); the nine bathroom tones; that the cube
ties the other rooms at the room's own mode width (4.4 Hz) and not below.
**Verified against sources here:** Billah and Scanlan p. 120 (OCR of the
ketchum.org scan: "was 42 mph … 12 c/m, or 0.2 Hz … close to 1 Hz, wholly out
of synch … natural vortex shedding was not the cause of the collapse"), and the
balance clause (Belykh and others 2021, open copy at PubMed Central: "the
primary objective of pedestrians walking on vibrating ground is to remain
balanced"; Discussion: "negative damping due to pedestrians' attempts to
maintain balance"; read through a page-fetch extraction, to be re-read in the
saved copy). **Not verifiable here:** Dallard pp. 22 to 23, the Helmholtz
German card text, and the board report's pages (the research pass's copies are
outside the repository).

**What changed overall.**
- Maya's nine majors, in her words, share one cause: the P3 cut moved the
  sentence the next line needed from the voice to a lower-third, and a viewer
  cannot read one while watching the picture. Restored in the voice, at a cost
  of 9.3 s, paid for inside the budget: 08_7 says what damping is, 06_7 says a force is a
  stretch times a stiffness, 13_4 says "eighteen of one pattern and four of the
  other", 11_1 says a bigger room lets sound linger longer, 07_5 says why the
  heavier deck is slower, 11_3 asks for "exactly one tone", 05_3 says "along the
  span". The cut stays at 14.98 min (899.4 s by the estimate).
- Four defects only a recomputation could show, all real: "the economy keeps
  three tenths of what it makes" is true of equal amounts only (D4, M-30); the
  split's dot-product recipe fails for patterns not at right angles, so 13_4's
  shares are solved, not projected (D3); "good enough for how far it sags" is
  true of one smooth load and not of a sharp one (D5); "the own patterns shift a
  little" holds for the lowest few modes only (R-04).
- Three source gaps, each flagged by two to four readers: "to keep their
  balance" (sourced now), the Helmholtz card (replaced by the recorded p. 147
  sentence), Billah and Scanlan p. 120 (re-read).
- One picture that read as the whole list when it was not (the attic is a
  two-dimensional slice, R-05, EN-2), and one ordinal that was wrong in three
  files (the factory is the owner's second image, R-26).
- The protected phrases were not touched (23 of 23, `./x eigen_engineering
  protected`). Five findings would have changed one and are flagged for the
  owner below.
