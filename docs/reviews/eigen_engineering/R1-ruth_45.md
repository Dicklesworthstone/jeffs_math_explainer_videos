## Comprehension check

- **01** A footbridge whose bounce had been checked swayed 7 cm sideways under walkers and was shut within two days; what the footsteps found was a motion the structure owns, and one calculation is promised to find such patterns in bridges, rooms and economies.
- **02** Holding two spring-coupled carts in place takes pushes on both, so moves-to-pushes is a 2 by 2 table and no part can be solved alone; but moving both together returns pushes in the same pattern.
- **03** Sweep the move round a circle and the push lines up with it in exactly two directions (together, times 1; against, times 3); those are the eigenvectors and eigenvalues, and this picture carries the rest.
- **04** Asked backwards, a push of (1, 0) is half of each pattern, each half is divided by its own stiffness, and the pieces add to (2/3, 1/3): two coupled equations become two divisions.
- **05** A 24 m steel truss is 21 springs assembled into a sparse symmetric 21 by 21 table; one 100 kN load moves all 21 directions, and five sag the middle 18 mm with 450 kN in the top chord.
- **06** That table has 21 orthogonal patterns from 3.12 to 1,226 MN/m; dividing each share of the load by its stiffness lets the softest nearly reproduce the midspan sag, while the member forces need many more.
- **07** With mass the problem is Kφ = ω²Mφ and each mode swings at one frequency (5.9, 11.8, 14.9 Hz; 4.3 with the deck doubled), which is where engineers use the decomposition daily.
- **08** Tacoma was a 0.2 Hz twisting mode fed by wind forces tied to the motion, and London was sideways modes fed by walkers whose push grew with the sway; neither was a matter of strength.
- **09** Air in a tube is the same chain of masses and springs, its eigenvectors are standing waves, Helmholtz called such tones Eigentöne in 1863, and c/2L gives a bathroom 68.6 Hz.
- **10** A hall-sized box has 1,964,040 modes below 1 kHz, each blurred about 1.2 Hz by a 1.9 s decay, so above about 20 Hz they overlap and the eigen-picture is true but useless.
- **11** So halls are designed with statistics and rays, single modes matter in small rooms (worst in a cube, where three coincide), and a room that is not a box is cut into cells and solved as the same eigenproblem.
- **12** An input table turns "made" into "used up"; the even mix comes back as 0.7 of itself, so it is an eigenvector, and nonnegativity guarantees the largest eigenvalue has a makeable one.
- **13** Meeting a demand means making it, plus what that uses, and so on; the series sums to (I − C)⁻¹d exactly when the largest eigenvalue is below one (80 of each here) and diverges at 1.1.
- **14** The factory problem has a table too, but it asks for the best point of a polygon and is answered at a corner, (40, 20) for 460, by linear programming: not every table question is an eigen-question.
- **15** Symmetric tables always have a full orthogonal set (K = QΛQᵀ); the economy's has its leading pattern by nonnegativity; the idea stops at defective or rotating tables, overlapping modes and nonlinearity; the bridge returns with dampers.
