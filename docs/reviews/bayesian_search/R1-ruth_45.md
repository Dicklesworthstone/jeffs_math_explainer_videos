## Comprehension check

The one sentence I would say to a friend after each chapter.

| chapter | my sentence |
|---|---|
| 01 the question | It asks how you find a lost submarine in time and plants one odd idea, that a search which finds nothing still changes what you know; the idea is only true when the search could have seen the thing, and the film says so in chapter 13. |
| 02 ninety-nine | The Scorpion went down on 22 May 1968 with all 99 men, five days before anyone knew to look, and even to find the wreck the Navy could not sweep the whole patch: at the strip width measured afterwards one pass needed about 4,600 sea miles of towing and the five months managed about 1,000. |
| 03 a map of chances | A map of chances is made from stories and their weights: 60 dots from one story and 40 from the other land as 40, 30, 20, 10, which is the law of total probability done by counting. |
| 04 ten thousand dots | The Navy's map was made the same way (nine weighted scenarios, 10,000 simulated runs, counted by cell); its top cell E5 held one run in eight and the wreck lay in the cell touching E5's corner, within 260 yards of its edge, so the map was good and still was not enough. |
| 05 a search can miss | Because a search can miss, the cell to search is the one where the chance she is there times the chance of seeing her is largest, so the canyon cell with 40% loses to the flat cell with 30%, ten against twenty. |
| 06 nothing found | An empty search of B puts out the 20 of its 30 dots that the search would have caught; counting the 80 left drops B to one in eight and lifts every unsearched cell by the same factor 5/4. That is conditioning, and it is the centre of the film. |
| 07 Bayes' rule | The recount has a name: multiply each cell by the chance of an empty search if she were there, rescale, repeat; with A at a half but in the canyon, the next search is still C. |
| 08 where next | Taking, every time, the search with the best chance of a find is provably never behind any other order (Chew 1967), under conditions; on the 1968 map a model search by the rule reaches 53% by its twentieth search, by the map's own reckoning, against under 1% for a sweep that starts in the empty corner. |
| 09 the second pass | Track that lands at random overlaps itself, so one cell's worth covers 63% on average, two 86%, three 95% (Koopman); two independent two-in-three passes see her eight times in nine and no number makes it certain. |
| 10 adrift | A person in the water drifts and each dot drifts with its own guess, so the cloud's area grows as the square of its radius, the same flight is spread four times thinner when the radius doubles, and in the film's model its chance of a sighting falls from 93% at one hour to 13% at twelve. |
| 11 twelve hours | The Coast Guard runs the same loop on drifting particles; in 2013 its first map for John Aldridge was almost thirty miles off because the assumed time of his fall was wrong, and it was new evidence from the boat, fed in by people, that moved the map to where a helicopter found him alive. |
| 12 the silence | For Air France 447, analysts mixed three weighted ingredients, folded in four empty searches and, doubting the beacons, drew a second map that ignored a month of silent listening; the wreck was found about a week into the resumed search, near the centre, in a high-chance area of that second map. |
| 13 how well they looked | At sea in 1968 the sonar was credited with 800 feet when against the hull it was worth nothing, so the cell holding the wreck was scored 94% searched when 65% was nearer the truth; an empty search teaches only as much as it could have seen. |
| 14 four maps | **Blocker.** The sentence the chapter hands me is "the method works because it never needs the first map to be right". I cannot repeat it: it is false as stated (R1), and in the three stories the repair came from people (widths re-measured after the find, a second map, a re-run with new evidence), not from empty searches moving dots. |

### Bayesian reading, line by line

The location chances are spoken throughout as things we hold about a wreck that is in one place ("she is at one of them", 06_4), and the weights as judgments ("Say sixty to forty", 03_5).
