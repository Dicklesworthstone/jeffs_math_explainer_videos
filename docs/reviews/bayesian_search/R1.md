# R1 dispositions — bayesian_search (Video H)

Round R1 on the storyboard (plan §5.2), 2026-10-13, against the storyboard at
commit `38e7150`. Reviews (verbatim, but for scratch-folder paths, which
are replaced by words): `R1-maya_14.md` (M), `R1-dev_28.md`
(D), `R1-ruth_45.md` (R), `R1-engine_feasibility.md` (E),
`R1-creative_director.md` (C). 159 findings: 2 blockers, 46 major, 83 minor,
28 delights.

Dispositions: **fixed** (in `explainer_corpus/bayesian_search/docs/storyboard.md`;
beat ids are the revised ones), **answered** (why not, one sentence), or
**deferred** (to a bead: `.5` is the script and `docs/description.md`, `.6`
the build, `.8` narration and score). Gate G2r: no finding without a
disposition; no open blocker.

**Blocker:** one, raised three times (R1, D1; M7 as a major). The ending said
the method "never needs the first map to be right". Dev and Ruth each ran
it: the update only multiplies, so a cell given no dots stays at zero after
any number of empty searches, and every story in the video was put right
from outside the loop (widths re-measured, a second map, new evidence).
Fixed at 14_3 and 14_4: the question is "what does the method ask of you?"
and the answer is a first map that leaves room for the truth, an honest
account of how well you looked, and a new map when evidence says a story was
wrong. On screen: "the update multiplies and never adds: a place with no
dots can never gain one". Pinned by `a_cell_with_no_dots_never_gains_one`
and `F-U.zero_stays_zero`.

**What changed overall.**

- **The proof, in place of an assertion** (D5, R2, E2, M3). Chapter 08 said
  "it is a theorem" over a fan of 65,536 curves that could not be drawn.
  Dev and Ruth each offered the argument itself: every search of a cell puts
  out a fixed slice of the first hundred dots whatever happens in between,
  a cell's slices only shrink, so the best n searches are the n biggest
  slices and the rule takes them in order. That is now 08_2 and 08_3, drawn
  as bars, and a test (`the_rule_takes_the_biggest_slices_first`). The
  theorem's third condition, that each search is a fresh try, is spoken
  there and at 09_5.
- **Air France 447, held to its source** (R10). The draft drew the wreck
  bright on the second map and dim on the first and called the silence
  "trusted too far". The paper says the investigators planned from the
  first map, and its two figures are both dark near the centre; I read
  both again. 12_5, 12_6 and 14_2 now say only what the paper does.
- **Dots that can be counted** (R7, D2, M16). The second search of the
  small sea would put out thirteen and a third dots; every dot now splits
  in three first ("more dots, same map", 07_5), and thirds are shown as
  thirds.
- **A made-up sea that is plainly made up** (C4), a toy ship that is "it"
  and never "she", and no captioned stories that could pass for the Navy's.
- **The 99** (C1, C2, C3, R8): no dot dims as they are named; the stage
  clears and chapter 02's card returns; nothing sounds after either
  sentence; no countdown ever runs over a real person; and 02_1 no longer
  says "This happened" after "Men are trapped".
- **Palomares told where its idea is used** (M8, C26), in chapter 04, not
  met for the first time in the recap.
- **One number for one thing in chapter 09** (M4, D7, R20): sixty-three
  percent at one coverage, two in three at a little more, and the quiz on
  nine dots the viewer can count.
- **What this cost.** About 50 seconds: the G2 estimate went from 15.0 to
  15.8 minutes, over the order's 11 to 15. The storyboard names the cuts
  the owner can take. Two things reviewers asked for were written and then
  cut for length: a sentence at 02_4 promising the living (M11), and "the
  boat motored on without him" (M29). The race to the wreck's own cell
  (thirty-fifth search against eighty-first) is gone, because a cruder plan
  wins that race (D6) and its percentages were said of "her" (R5).
