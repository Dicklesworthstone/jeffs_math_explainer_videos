## What I checked, and how

- **Length and clock.** I re-ran the gate's estimator on the packet's storyboard in a scratch script (2.68 words a second, 0.6 s a beat, 2.5 s a hold, listen windows as written, 2 s per ⟨predict⟩). Result: 902.2 s, 15.04 min, 2,006 spoken words over 87 beats; 14 question beats (6 countdowns, 8 to carry); 13 holds (32.5 s); 8 listen windows (41 s, of which 34 s is countdown). That matches the storyboard's 15.0. Every time in this review is from that clock. Some chapter headings look stale against it (02 says 67 s, I get 69.3). 12 s of the total is the 2 s question allowance on the six countdown beats, which the animatic plays in addition to the ring.
- **The spectacle on that clock.** The dots, wordless, at 0:11. The rain of ten thousand dots at 3:02 to 3:14. Then four minutes on the four-cell sea. The fan of 65,536 orders at 7:14, the two-map race at 7:25 with no gap, the lanes at 8:13, the drifting cloud at 9:20, the cloud moved south at 11:06, the three maps merging at 11:41, the dots relighting at 13:05, the cold open returning at 14:33. The six quiz questions end, and their countdowns begin, at about 2:22, 4:23, 5:29, 6:37, 8:50 and 9:46; there is none after that.
- **Colour.** CIEDE2000 between the eight role colours and the background from their hexes (sRGB, D65), with my own implementation, which reproduces six published reference pairs to four places. Three pairs fall under 20 (C8). I tried nine alternative palettes with the same code. The crate has no palette test yet (I searched for one); Video F's storyboard pins every pair at 24 or more.
- **Sound.** The identity (D Dorian, 76 BPM, pad brightness 0.25, vibraphone, pluck; motif 1, 2, 1, 4; resolution 3, 2, 1) against plan §7.2 and §7.3 and against the storyboard's sound plan. In the engine: a storyboard `hold` is a `music_breath` in the animatic; a breath releases the duck fully from about 0.3 s in (the test `a_two_and_a_half_second_breath_is_heard_and_the_next_line_is_ducked`); the identity's duck is 8 dB; an unlabelled mark is a reveal cue; `tick`, `whoosh`, `thin`, `silence` and `silence_end` exist as cue kinds.
- **Pictures I recomputed.** 02_5: one cell takes 6,076 ÷ 200 = 30.4 passes of 0.84 miles, 25.52 miles of track; × 180 cells = 4,594 miles; 1,026 ÷ 4,594 = 22.3%, which is 40.2 of the 180 cells. 06: after the first search the lit dots are 40, 10, 20, 10, and stretched to a hundred they end at 50, 62.5, 87.5, 100. 07_6: the third choice is A (15, 10, 6.7, 10). 08_3: a two-in-three search of E5 puts out 833 of its 1,250 dots and raises every other cell by a factor of 1.09. 08_5: F6 is the 81st cell in reading order (5 rows of 15, plus 6). 10_5: four discs of radius r do not fit in a disc of radius 2r (four need 2.41 r; two fit exactly); the same length of track re-laid over twice the diameter has half as many legs, spaced four times as far apart.
- **Focal points.** Counted by hand from the picture column: 16 beats put three or more things in motion or on arrival under one sentence (listed below).
- **Unchecked.** Anything rendered or heard: there are no frames and no audio at this stage, so every remark about legibility, motion or sound is a prediction, and every remark about what the engine will do is a reading of its code. The fact sheet's other numbers and all of its sources. Whether the engine can do what my fixes ask at 4K60, which is the engine reviewer's call. How the palette reads for colour-blind viewers. The wording of the narration, except where a picture depends on it.

## The key picture of each chapter

- **01_the_question.** One cell's dots going out under a teal strip while the rest of the map changes: the whole video in one move. As written its second half is "every other cell's share bar rises a little" on a full-frame grid, which nobody will see in the second it gets (C5, C6).
- **02_ninety_nine.** A date line that runs backwards five days past the day she was due and leaves one white ring and "99" on an empty stage.
- **03_a_map_of_chances.** Forty dots hanging above the four-cell sea while the ring drains, then falling ten to a cell; the quiz card as specified covers them (C10).
- **04_ten_thousand_dots.** Ten thousand dots raining onto the 1968 lattice and collapsing, in a ripple, into the 172 printed numbers, with the white ring just outside the hottest cell.
- **05_a_search_can_miss.** Ten of A's forty dots ringed against twenty of B's thirty, and B's border turning gold: the second-likeliest cell holds twice the rings.
- **06_nothing_found.** Twenty of B's dots going out and staying where they were, while A's forty do not move and A is nonetheless half the map. As written the "half" is a readout climbing, not something in the picture (C6).
- **07_bayes_rule.** None that owns the chapter: a name card, a table, a formula, a quiz and then, for five seconds, a loop sharing the frame with four other things. The loop should be it (C11).
- **08_where_next.** A gold curve riding the top edge of a grey fan of 65,536 orders; then two copies of the Navy's map, one swept along its empty top row and one hopping to E5. Two images in 54 s with no breath between them (C12).
- **09_the_second_pass.** The same hundred lanes laid twice: edge to edge, filling the cell; and where they land, leaving dark gaps.
- **10_adrift.** One point blooming into two thousand dots that drift and spread, and then the same flight track over the cloud at 1, 3, 6 and 12 hours.
- **11_twelve_hours.** A cloud of dots by the coast and one white point thirty miles south of it. The payoff, the dots moving onto the point, is written as "redrawn": a cut where the idea is a move (C23).
- **12_the_silence.** One disc drawn twice, the white ring bright on the map that set the silence aside and dim on the map that trusted it, with a listening track lying across it.
- **13_how_well_they_looked.** Ninety-four of F6's hundred dots out with the ring among the six still lit, and then dots coming back on until 35 are lit: the only time in the video that a dot relights.
- **14_four_maps.** The cold open's picture returning with its names. Before it, 46 s of table; after it, with no pause, the 99 (C15, C26).

## Straight answers to the questions put to this lane

- **Is the hero picture set up early, recognisable throughout and paid off?** Set up at 0:11 and named at 1:58 ("Each dot is one place she could be"). Present in 12 of 14 chapters. Three things blur it on the way: the half of it that says "every other cell rises" is drawn five different ways and, at the centre of the video, not drawn at all (C6); "lit" means "still possible" in the map and "seen" in 05_2 and 09_6 (C7); and in chapter 08, the one time the method runs on the real map, the dots are absent and the map is heat only (C12). The payoff at 14_7 is the right one and has no room (C15).
- **The cold open.** The owner's question as the first words is right, and the storyboard is honest about it: the truth is told at 0:42. The picture after it is the right picture. It is not silent, though the brief says "no words yet for what it is": a 15-word sentence runs over six events in 5.6 s. It needs two and a half seconds to itself, a named grid and a dot count that cannot be read as the crew (C5).
- **Do showcase features carry ideas or decorate?** They carry. Ten thousand particles are the Navy's ten thousand runs; the grid of numbers is the printed figure; the live percentages are counts of lit dots; the fan is the theorem; the lanes are Koopman's model; the spreading cloud is why hours matter. There is no 3D and no camera route, and I agree: a lit seabed is the dramatized picture the brief rules out. No streamlines, and I agree with that too: one drawn current field would say the wind is known, which is the opposite of 10_2's point. What decorates is small: counters and bars that repeat a picture already on screen (02_2's count-up of days, 02_5's and 05_3's bars, 08_4's bars) and gold used as a generic highlight (C9). The empty row is sonification: no sound teaches anything except how long is left (C14).
- **Sound.** The plan is short and its instincts are good (a put-out dot is silent; the motif is left open at the title). Three things need deciding before the score bead starts. What happens after "All ninety-nine men were lost": the engine's default is to bring the bed up 8 dB (C2). What the countdown sounds like in a video that opens on "time is running out" (C3). And what the search sound means, since as written it would fire dozens of times a minute in three chapters (C14). Risks to audition: the countdown's landing is the resolution figure, six times before the ending (C16); a dark pad over a 37 Hz bass is the sound of a submarine film (C17).
- **Pacing of the spectacle.** No stretch reaches five minutes without a set piece, but 3:14 to 7:14 is four minutes on four cells, and the loop at 6:56 is the natural lift (C11). One pair is back to back with no breath: the fan and the race (C12). 7:14 to 9:30 is the densest stretch (fan, race, lanes, cloud). All thirteen breaths fall on finished pictures at chapter ends; none falls on a picture that is moving (C14).
- **Tone.** Most of it is right, and right in ways that took thought: no hull, no wreckage, no water; the loss told by a date line; a ring that goes out at "Nobody knew that yet"; dots that make no sound; red kept for wrong estimates and never used for the dead; the bomb, the bodies and the cause left out. The places where a picture or a sound would dramatize the loss or blur what is known are few and specific: dots dimming as the 99 are named (C1); the bed swelling after the sentence (C2); a ticking ring over a person in the water (C3); two captioned "stories" that read as the Navy's (C4); green on the wreck (C9); a white cross beside "228" and a label about bodies (C22, C24); the sub-bass (C17).
- **The ending.** Right in content: the opening picture in its exact form, then the 99, claimed for nothing. Wrong in spacing: the intellectual payoff, the sentence for the dead, a living person found and the musical resolution fall inside 25 seconds with one hold among them (C15).
