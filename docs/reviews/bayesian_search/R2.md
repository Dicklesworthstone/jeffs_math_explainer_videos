## Method

Four fresh readers, each with its own persona prompt, read the same packet
(stage rules, brief, script, storyboard, description draft, fact sheet), and
none read another's review or R1. Maya, Dev and Ruth are the project's
standing personas (`maya_14`, `dev_28`, `ruth_45`; Ruth's prompt includes
`ruth_audit.md`, the mathematician's checklist). The fourth reader is a
search-and-rescue planner, fifteen years at a rescue coordination centre,
trained on the Coast Guard's planning software and tables, who teaches new
watchstanders and is watching to decide whether to let them watch. The persona
prompt was written for this round: what the reader needs (nothing that would
teach a trainee something false about how searches are planned; respect for
the people lost and the people who searched; the standard words in their
standard sense), what the reader notices (a sweep width treated as a hard
edge, a detection chance from a formula for randomly laid track, "the map
finds the thing" where a person found it, hours and survival, a drift error
said to be one thing and modelled as another), and an instruction that a
number not recomputed is "unchecked", never "fine".

Findings are proposals. A finding that a computation or a source could settle
was settled here before it was accepted (the next section). The
`modes-of-reasoning-project-analysis` discipline was applied in its lean form:
one named question (which claims are false, or true and misleading to a
tenth-grader or a search-and-rescue professional, so that a revision changes
words before narration), independent readers, claims audited by someone other
than their author, checked nulls allowed (each reviewer returned several
"checked, nothing to report" lines) and no quotas. Its run-directory ledger
was not built, because this file is the record.

**Provenance of the review files.** The four files were not written by the
reviewers: each reviewer returned its review as text and the producer saved
it verbatim; each file says so in a header note, and Ruth's and the
planner's add a line of context. Ruth's first run was cut off by a usage limit and left
nothing; her file is the second, complete run. Ruth reports reading "all 95
script lines"; `script.tsv` has 82 (she may have counted table rows of the
packet). The planner's own parallel-lane figures (S1, S11) are an
illustration of hers, not a source, and are not on screen.

## What was verified by recomputation here

A script of the producer's own (not `facts.rs`, not `oracle.py`), run on the
oracle's transcription of the 1968 map (Figure 2) as data. Every number the
reviewers gave was reproduced:

- **Row by row from row 1: 0.76% at 20 searches.** The first twenty cells
  hold 114 of the 10,000 dots. From row 5: 22.3%. From row 12: 0.19%. Column
  by column from A: 10.3%. A random order averages **6.6%** (10th to 90th
  percentile 2.3 to 12.6; 26.7% at 81 searches). Normalised over the 180
  cells it covers instead of all 10,000 runs: 0.9, 49.8 and 55.9 (1,100 dots
  lie outside the grid). So "less than one chance in a hundred" is true of the
  row order on screen and flatters the contrast by about nine times; "most of
  that is the map itself" is the honest part (6.6, then 44.3, then 49.8).
- **A thin place is reached late.** F6, planted at 85 dots, is first searched
  at search 30 (at 10 dots, 160; at 1 dot, 429). 40 of the 180 cells hold no
  dots and are never searched. All three in the oracle now.
- **One sea mile is 6,076.115 feet**, so the "6,076 feet each" printed beside
  the total gives 4,593 and not 4,594; one cell is 25.52 sea miles (26 × 180 =
  4,680); at 800 feet one pass over the patch is 1,148 miles, and the 1,026
  towed is 89% of that.
- **10_4:** from hour 1 to hour 12 the radius grows 4.41 times and the area
  19.5 times (6.6 to 128.7 sq mi), so "twice the radius is four times the
  area" is the principle and not the ratio of the two circles. If the model's
  0.3 were a probable error with a containing circle twice as wide, the four

- **09_3:** in 1,000 runs of random strips the single run drawn (66.5%) sits
  1.7 standard deviations above the mean of 63.4; the band runs 57.1 to 69.9.
- **13:** F6's chance by the ship's numbers 0.189%, by the estimated ones

## Flagged for the owner

- **S4 (open; major, unchecked): what is the 0.3?** The model's cloud edge is
  1 sea mile plus 0.3 times the distance drifted, with every dot inside. The
  planner recalls the manual's drift error as a probable error, which is then
  multiplied by a factor to contain the object. If so, the containing circle
  is about twice the radius and the four figures become 49, 23, 10 and 3. The
  trend and "That is why hours matter" survive; the magnitudes would not. The
  narration says "in this model", and 10_4's label now says what the model
  is ("the cloud a uniform disc whose edge holds every dot: 1 mile plus 0.3 of
  the distance drifted"). Needs Frost and Stone (2001) or the manual page; recorded in
  `decisions.md` as pending owner, and as an open item in the description.
- **The 2-in-5 parenthesis (D20).** The description's "about two in five
  rather than 13 in 100" depended on a radius definition the packet did not
  give; it is dropped from the description until S4 is settled.
- **Length (M25, D22).** The planner and three personas would cut 08_4 and
  08_5 first if the owner must cut about 50 seconds; they are also where
  D1, D2 and S7 were found. No chapter is over 85 s with the fixes (G2
  estimate). The owner's call; nothing was cut here.
- **"Just above the sea floor" (D17).** One word, "near", would remove the
  claim; the source row is unchecked. Not changed.
