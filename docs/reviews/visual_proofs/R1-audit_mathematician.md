## 4. Findings

Severity: **blocker** = something said or shown is false, or a proof has a hole; **major** = a stated claim, card or quiz that a careful viewer or reviewer would reject, fixable in words; **minor** = precision, ordering, traceability.

### Major

**A1. major. 07_2, and the note "The quiz is honest" under chapter 07 in `explainer_corpus/visual_proofs/docs/storyboard.md`.**
The note says "with whole cubes no number of stacks makes a box (90 cubes have no box with every side at least 4)". The parenthesis is true for three stacks, but the universal is false: **six whole four-level stacks tile a 4 x 5 x 9 box with no cutting** (exact cover search, witness extracted and re-verified in `c07c_six_stacks_witness.py`; 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 stacks tile none of their boxes). Consequences: (i) "how many of these stacks make a solid box, if cubes may be cut in half?" has the correct answers 3, 6, 9, ..., not only 3; (ii) the rule "cut in half" is not defined (free dissection would let one stack become a box of half cells). The spoken answer "Three." is true as "the fewest, with the top step's cubes sliced"; the question is not.
**Fix.** Ask for the fewest and name the rule: "Your turn. Guess the fewest copies of this stack that make a solid box, if the cubes of the top step may be sliced in half." Replace the note with: "with whole cubes three stacks cannot (90 cubes have no box with every side at least 4); six can, a 4 x 5 x 9 box found by exhaustive search; the half cut is what lets three do it; one or two stacks (30, 60 cubes) are under the 64 of the least box round a stack". Add a fact `F-S.six.box` = `4x5x9` if the six-stack tiling is kept anywhere.

**A2. major. 10_4 "Guess a total a thousandth short of one, and ten pieces pass it. A millionth short, and twenty pieces pass it. So no number smaller than one is big enough to be the total."**
The conclusion is universal (every number below one is passed) and it is drawn with "So" from two instances, the exact move chapter 02 takes apart. The reason (each piece halves the gap, so the gap is 1/2^n and is below any size you name) is on the 10_2 beat ("the gap ... exactly the size of the last piece") and in the lower-third and the card, but it is not spoken at the point of inference. Both instances are correct (10 and 20; 9 and 19 do not pass).
**Fix.** After the two guesses add one sentence: "Each piece takes half of what is left, so the gap is halved every time, and it drops below any size you name." Then "So no number smaller than one is big enough to be the total." Keep the picture of the red band.

**A3. major. 11_6 card Shown line.**
"three colours share the square equally, short of all of it by the uncut corner" is true at any finite stage and is not the theorem the chapter proves. It has no range, no 1/3 (the quiz answer), no mention of the three equal strips, and no mention that the corner goes below any size, which is the step that turns the finite fact into the endless sum. Every other card states its range and conclusion. The lower-third at 11_6 ("a finite sum and its remainder, the same remainder, a third of the last piece") is also hard to parse; its content is Proposition 23: A + B + ... + Z + Z/3 = (4/3)A.
**Fix.** Shown: "for every whole number n from 1, after n cuts each colour holds (1 - 1/4^n) / 3 of the square, a third short by a third of the uncut corner, and the corner goes below any size, so 1/4 + 1/16 + 1/64 + ... = 1/3." Assumed: "the three squares of each size are equal, ticked, with 'each slides onto the other'; the corner is cut into three equal strips". Lower-third: "Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola, Prop. 23: a finite sum plus a third of its last piece is exactly 4/3 of its first."

### Minor

**A4. minor. 03_5 "The same picture works a hundred layers on."** From the 5 by 5 square this is layer 105 (104 + 104 + 1 = 209 tiles, 105 x 105 = 11025), but the next sentence and the picture are the hundredth layer (99 + 99 + 1 = 199, 100 x 100 = 10000), 95 layers on. **Fix:** "The same picture works all the way to the hundredth layer."

**A5. minor. 03_6 "A picture that shows why is a proof."** The vocabulary entry defines a proof as a picture that shows why and says what it took for granted; the spoken sentence keeps only the first half, and chapter 14's picture also looks as if it shows why. **Fix:** "A picture that shows why, and says what it took for granted, is a proof."

**A6. minor. 06_3 picture: "the violet 5 slides out sideways and comes back above the pink 1 ... likewise copper 2 and blue 4; the green 3 stays".** Re-tiling a 15-long arm by two 6-blocks and one 3-block puts the 3 at [0,3), [6,9) or [12,15) only; the green starts at [3,6), and a violet "above the pink 1" occupies [1,6). Brute force over all orders found no arrangement with pairs 1 + 5 and 2 + 4 contiguous and the green left in place. As written the choreography cannot be drawn. **Fix:** "the green 3 is the one without a partner: it slides to the end of the arm" (final order 1 + 5, 2 + 4, 3), or say "stays out of the pairing".

**A7. minor. 06_3 letters line and the cited keys.** (a) "pieces 1, 2, ..., k - 1, each with its partner makes k" needs "partner" defined for the middle piece of an even cube (its twin in the other arm, the half + half brace). (b) `F-K.arm.7` and `F-K.arm.8` are cited but not defined in `explainer_corpus/visual_proofs/docs/fact-sheet.md`, and the row's "facts to add: build notes" points to a Build notes list that does not contain them (it lists only `F-H.pieces_to.tenth`, `.hundredth` and the 11_5 strips). **Fix:** add `F-K.arm.7` = `7x21` with `F-K.arm.7.slabs` = `3`, `F-K.arm.8` = `8x28` with `.slabs` = `7/2`, and the line "partner: the piece k - j in the same arm, or for j = k/2 its twin in the other arm".

**A8. minor. 06_4 Assumed "its arms are k - 1 half slabs".** Each arm is k - 1 half slabs; both arms are k - 1 *whole* slabs. As one clause it reads either way, and the narration at 06_3 says "one slab fewer". **Fix:** "each arm is k - 1 half slabs, so the two arms are k - 1 slabs and the corner is the last".

**A9. minor. 06_5 "every cube is a run of odd numbers".** The rule "k odd numbers from k(k - 1) + 1" is displayed for k <= 5 only; its reason (the run is centred on k^2: (k^2 - k + 1) ... (k^2 + k - 1), k terms of average k^2) is not on screen, so as drawn the "second proof" of the storyboard note is a check of five cases. **Fix:** under the rule add one line "k odd numbers centred on k squared: k x k^2 = k^3", or state that the thick layer is k slabs (06_3) so its thin layers add to k^3, and that the start comes from 1 + ... + (k - 1) odd numbers already used (chapter 04).

**A10. minor. 06_6 "The slabs cut in half are much younger, from nineteen eighty-five."** Says first appearance. The fact sheet's S5 is a Crossref record plus a secondary description (printed page not seen), S6 notes earlier pictures by Golomb (1965) and Love (1977) known only by description. **Fix:** "Alan Fry published a picture with the slabs cut in half in nineteen eighty-five."

**A11. minor. 07_4 picture note "Every slab is a full 4 by 5 board."** True for the four lower slabs; the top slab holds 10 of 20 cells (it is the half that 08_2 fills). **Fix:** "the four lower slabs are full 4 by 5 boards; the top has ten of the twenty cells". (Related, no fix needed: on the last step the "L" is a single tile and only pink takes it.)

**A12. minor. 08_4 "With any number of levels, the box is that many cubes wide, one more deep, and that many and a half tall."** Said, with the letters formula, one chapter before the step is shown (09_1 then asks "Why would five fit?"). For about thirty seconds the video does what chapter 02 warns against: a formula from one case. **Fix:** in 08_4 say "With four levels ..." and the numbers only, and put the letters line at 09_4 after the induction, or say "If this goes on working for any number of levels, ...".

**A13. minor. 09_5 card.** (a) Shown "three stacks of n levels fill a box n by n + 1 by n + 1/2" omits "with the top step's cubes sliced in half and the halves turned into the empty half"; without it the statement is false for whole cubes (the misconception row 07 defuses exactly that). (b) Assumed omits the base case ("height one is three cubes in a 1 x 2 x 3/2 box"), which 06_4's card does list for its induction. **Fix:** Shown: "for every whole number n from 1, three stacks of n levels, with the cubes of the top step sliced in half and turned into the empty half, fill a box n by n + 1 by n + 1/2". Assumed: add "height one is three cubes in a 1 by 2 by 1 1/2 box, ticked".

**A14. minor. 10_5 card.** Shown "the pieces fill the square up to a gap the size of the last piece, which never closes and goes below any size" has no range ("after any number of pieces") and does not say the conclusion 1/2 + 1/4 + ... = 1. Assumed "every piece lies inside what was left" does not say each piece is *exactly half* of what was left, which is what makes the gap 1/2^n (the close-up shows it, the sentence does not). **Fix:** Shown: "after n pieces, for every n from 1, the square is filled up to a gap the size of the last piece, 1/2^n; the gap never closes and goes below any size, so 1/2 + 1/4 + ... = 1". Assumed: "each piece is exactly half of what was left, so it lies inside it and the pieces never overlap or leave the square, ticked".

**A15. minor. 12_7 card.** Shown "the cut square and the cut cube, for any two lengths" does not say what is shown; Assumed is absent. **Fix:** Shown: "for any two lengths a and b: a square of side a + b is a^2 + 2ab + b^2 and a cube is a^3 + 3a^2b + 3ab^2 + b^3, each block counted once". Add: "Assumed: cutting each side once cuts the cube into boxes that fill it exactly; the size of a box is the product of its sides". Add a note that "long" and "short" name the two parts, so for a < b the "long" part is the smaller.

**A16. minor. 13_7 narration "But this picture took two things for granted. That the tilted shape is a square, and that the edges are straight. Both are true, because a right triangle's two sharp angles make a right angle."** The card lists three ticks (square; straight sides; pairs close into rectangles). The angle fact explains the first and third; "straight" is not a consequence of it, it is the premise (the legs lie end to end on the big square's straight side) that lets the angles add to a straight angle. **Fix:** "This picture took things for granted. That the tilted shape is a square: on the big square's straight side its corner sits between the two sharp angles, which add to a right angle, so the corner is a right angle. And that each pair closes into a rectangle, for the same reason."

**A17. minor. 14_3 "Same pieces, same outline, and a tile has vanished."** The two outlines are different polygons (bends at (8,3) and (5,2), areas 32 and 33); 14_2 says "looks the same", 14_3 states it. The narrator's one literally false sentence before 14_5. **Fix:** "Same pieces, an outline that looks the same, and a tile has vanished."

**A18. minor. 14_6 "17 pieces" and "whole tiles".** True (checked), but neither is a fact key in `explainer_corpus/visual_proofs/docs/fact-sheet.md`, `explainer_corpus/visual_proofs/src/facts.rs` or `explainer_corpus/visual_proofs/oracle.py`; only the area (`F-L.sliver` = 1) and thickness are. **Fix:** add `F-L.sliver.pieces` = `17` and `F-L.sliver.fold` = the 17 integer slides into (7,1), computed from the exact clipping of the parallelogram (0,0) (5,2) (13,5) (8,3), with a test that they do not overlap and sum to area 1.
