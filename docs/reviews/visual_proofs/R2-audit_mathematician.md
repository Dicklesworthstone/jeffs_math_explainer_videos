## 3. Problems, worst first

All three are minor. There is no blocker and no major.

**P-1 (minor), line 06_6, spoken.** "the rule" has no clear antecedent. The last rule named in 06_5 is Nicomachus's runs of odd numbers; what al-Karaji proved (Heath) is the sum-of-cubes theorem stated at 06_4, using gnomons. A listener can hear "Al-Karaji proved Nicomachus's rule", which is not what the source says. Replacement, same line id, obeying the voice rules:

06_6: Al-Karaji proved that the cubes up to any number make a square, using L-shaped layers, around the year one thousand. A version of the picture with the slabs cut in half was published in nineteen eighty-five.

(Only the first sentence changes. It is spoken, so decide before narration; it is not a falsehood either way.)

**P-2 (minor), line 11_6, on-screen lower-third.** The statement of Proposition 23 drops its hypothesis. Replacement text for the lower-third (letters allowed on screen):

"Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola, Prop. 23: if each piece is a quarter of the one before, a finite sum plus a third of its last piece is exactly 4/3 of its first"

**P-3 (minor), line 13_7, on-screen lower-third.** "No source we found says who first drew this picture" is defensible, but the project's own S13 row records that the earliest printing it found (Loomis, citing the Mathematical Monthly of 1859) is credited to A. D. Wheeler, and a viewer who finds that will read the sentence as wrong. Either keep the sentence and add the printing, for example: "No source we found says who first drew this picture. The earliest printing we found, in 1859, is credited to A. D. Wheeler. A close cousin is in a third-century Chinese commentary on the Zhoubi suanjing", or keep it as is and rely on the description, which already says this. Either is acceptable; the first is more honest on screen.

## 4. Rejected tempting objections

1. "Cut two slabs in half" may be more than needed. Rejected: exhaustive search over every placement shows each of the two cube and the four cube needs one cut and none of the one, three, five cubes does, so two is the minimum (a02).
2. 05_4 "How many will have to be cut" could be zero with other cuts or other layouts. Rejected: no tiling of the L (width 4, arms 4 by 6) by four whole 4 by 4 slabs exists, so at least one must be cut, and one halved slab suffices (a02).
3. 04_5 "at any height" fails at one step. Rejected: n = 1 is two tiles making 1 by 2, the rule holds (a02, n = 1 to 200).
4. 06_3 "pair them up for any cube" breaks for even cubes because the middle piece has no partner. Rejected: the line says three is half of six; the two halves, one in each arm, pair with each other; the count k-1 slabs holds for k = 1 to 199 (a03).
5. 07_2 "fewest" is not a search over all packings. Rejected as a defect: under the stated rule the necessary-condition arithmetic is complete (every box holding a stack has both sides at least 4 and a volume of 30, 60 or 90 for one, two or three stacks; none for one and two, exactly one for three), it also covers vertical halving, and the construction for three exists (a04, a05). The full fill is shown, not assumed.
6. 09_2 says "one more slab goes underneath" (5 by 6, 30 cubes) while 09_3 says three slabs of 5 by 5. Rejected: they are the same 75 new cubes regrouped; the 5 by 6 base is the 5 by 5 under slab plus one row of the behind slab (a05).
7. 08_4 "if this goes on working" is only a conditional. Rejected: chapter 09 discharges it (C060 to C062).
8. 10_6 "exactly what it means" ignores the general definition of an infinite sum. Rejected: for positive pieces the video's description (nothing smaller is big enough, nothing passes one) is equivalent to the limit of partial sums; every piece is positive.
9. 11_6 "in ancient Greece" because Syracuse is in Sicily. Rejected: standard idiom for the Greek world, no viewer would be misled, and a change would be a needless rewording.
10. 12_6 "still eight blocks" fails when the cut is at the middle (long equals short) or at an end. Rejected: at the middle the eight blocks exist with equal parts (a06); "anywhere inside" excludes the end, where seven blocks lose volume (the fact sheet says so).
11. 12_7 "no one can build it in our space" is too strong because projections exist. Rejected: "build" means a faithful four-dimensional block; the card says "counted, not built".
12. 13_6 "every right triangle" fails for very unequal or equal legs. Rejected: exact for (1,1), (7,7), (1,60), (60,1) and rational legs (a07).
13. 13_7 the two sharp angles at the tilted corner might be the same angle twice. Rejected: they are the triangle's two different sharp angles (53.13 and 36.87 degrees for 3, 4; a07).
14. 14_5 "the long edge is not straight" holds only for the first arrangement. Rejected: the second arrangement's edge is the same two hypotenuses in the other order, bent the other way (a08).
15. 14_7 "the trick began with Paul Curry" ignores earlier dissection paradoxes (Hooper, Loyd). Rejected: the line is attributed ("Martin Gardner says") and Gardner credits Curry with the hole-in-the-figure version; the line does not claim the dissection idea began there.
16. 06_5 "Nicomachus noticed that every cube" might outrun the S2 row, which records only three cubes. Rejected after reading the primary text: Nicomachus gives the first six and "and so on".
17. 08_5 "published this picture" might outrun the S8 row (no printed figure seen). Rejected after seeing the printed figure: it is this picture (Nelsen p. 77, with the n+1/2 label).
18. 11_6 "Ajose and Nelsen" might be wrong because Nelsen's book credits Ajose alone. Rejected: the magazine record lists both authors (Crossref), which is what the line says.
19. Lower-thirds 02_6 and 08_4 do not print their range ("six points", "n from 1"). Rejected as a defect: the range is carried by the parenthesis in 02_6 and by the formula on screen in 08_4, and each sentence is true at every value where it is meaningful. If a polish pass is wanted, they would read "precisely: six points make 31 regions when ..." and "precisely: for every whole number n from 1, the textbook form is ...".
20. A forward reference at 04_6 ("the cubes' square is fifteen on a side") is not yet on screen as a number. Rejected: it is true (C029), only a forward reference.
21. Dating Al-Karaji "around the year one thousand". Rejected: Heath's "tenth-eleventh century" and the usual c. 1000 agree.
22. The 03_5 lower-third "the Greek word for this L-shaped layer" is too strong for Aristotle. Rejected: Aristotle uses gnomon in exactly this odd-number sense in Physics III.4; the lower-third claims only the word and cites the place.
