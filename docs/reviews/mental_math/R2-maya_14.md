- **02:** 35² is 3 × 4 = 12 then 25, because a strip 5 deep comes off the top of the 35 square and

  corner, put the zeros back by asking how big each piece must be; four easy numbers, but four

  waiting 13 × 14 = 182, so 135² = 18225.
- **06:** 10% is a tenth (move the point), 5% half of it, 20% double; 8% of $25 = 25% of $8 = $2
  because percent is cents on the dollar, and the swap helps with a friendly percent.
- **07:** get to a round number by halving and doubling (16 × 45 = 8 × 90) or by rounding a price
  and giving the cents back; each pays only when the round number is close and the fix small.
- **08:** 47 × 53 is the middle squared minus the distance squared (2500 − 9 = 2491); the strip
  that sticks out moves into the gap and leaves a 3 by 3 hole; it needs a whole middle easy to

  he didn't know how to square numbers near 50.
- **10:** 48² is the same strip move: 46 by 50 (2500 − 200, Bethe's 2300) plus 2 by 2; above
  fifty, 2500 + 300 + 9; the rule is 25 ± the distance, then the distance squared as two digits.
- **11:** 46² = 2116; the reading works within nine of fifty, because 12² = 144 spills into the
  hundreds (62² = 3844); Bethe was fast because every number was near something he knew.
- **12:** picking the trick first: a 20% tip is a tenth doubled ($9); 49 × 51 is the middle trick.
- **13:** 67 × 78 has nothing to grab: parts (slow, 5226), paper or a calculator; rounding first

- **14:** 55² = 3025 by both the fives and the near-fifty tricks, because those and the middle
  trick are one strip move; you need numbers you know and the habit of looking nearby.
