## Comprehension check

- **01_eighty_five_squared.** I was asked for 85² cold, shown that 8 × 9 = 72 followed by 25 gives 7,225, and told why the paper method is hard in the head (two rows and three carries to carry along, against one number); it matters because it sets the claim the rest has to earn, that each trick rests on a picture.
- **02_ends_in_five.** 35² is a square cut at 30 into a 30 × 30 block, two 30 × 5 strips and a 5 × 5 corner; turning one strip to stand beside the other makes the block 30 × 40, twelve hundreds, plus the 25, which is exactly why it is "the next number up": the two fives make one more ten.
- **03_your_turn.** Three quizzes (25², 65², 105²), then the general statement (10n + 5)² = 100 × n(n + 1) + 25 for every whole n, with its honest limit: the trick is only as easy as n(n + 1), and 135² needs 13 × 14, which is the reason for the next act.
- **04_by_parts.** 23 × 46 is a rectangle cut into four pieces (20 × 40, 20 × 6, 3 × 40, 3 × 6); you multiply the leading digits and put the size back by reasoning (tens times tens are hundreds), after first estimating 20 × 50 = 1,000; it matters because this one works for any product.
- **05_running_total.** Rather than store four pieces, keep one running total from the biggest piece down (800, 920, 1,040, 1,058), so that one number is held between steps; 13 × 14 = 182 then finishes 135² = 18,225.
- **06_the_middle.** Two numbers equally far from a middle multiply to the middle squared less the distance squared, because the strip that overhangs the 50-square fits the gap except for a 3 × 3 hole (47 × 53 = 2,500 − 9); it pays only when the middle is easy to square.
- **07_bethe.** Feynman's story from his book: Bethe gives 48² as 2,300 and then 2,304 while Feynman is still at the Marchant, by taking 100 × 2 off 2,500 and adding 2²; it gives squares near 50 a reason to exist and a human face.
- **08_near_fifty.** Why Bethe's rule works: below 50 two strips of 50 × d come off and the d × d corner, taken twice, goes back; above 50 two strips go on and the corner is new; so the square is 2,500, plus or minus 100 times the distance, plus the distance squared, read as "25 plus or minus the distance, then the distance squared in two digits".
- **09_your_turn_fifty.** Three quizzes (52², 46², 57²), then the limit: the two-pair reading is exact only from 41 to 59, because further out the distance squared spills into the hundreds (62² = 3,700 + 144), and Feynman's own summary of why Bethe was quick.
- **10_percents.** Percents built from the 10% block (half of it is 5%, double it is 20%), and x% of y = y% of x because both are xy ÷ 100, which helps only when one of the two is a friendly percent.
- **11_round_numbers.** Get to a round number and repair what you changed: × 5 is × 10 halved, halve one factor and double the other, × 9 is × 10 less one lot, and a .99 price is a round price less a few cents; each is the chapter 04 rectangle cut another way.
- **12_which_trick.** Five mixed problems where the first job is choosing the trick (75², 49 × 51, 54², 20% of $45, 6 × 49); it matters because recognising the structure, not the arithmetic, is the skill.
- **13_nothing_to_grab.** 67 × 78 has no five, no whole middle and no round neighbour; parts still gives 5,226 but slowly, so paper or a calculator is the right tool, and the habit to keep is the estimate 70 × 80 = 5,600, which catches a slipped decimal point.
- **14_near_something_you_know.** Three of the tricks are one identity, a² = (a − d)(a + d) + d², seen as one strip slid round a square; all of it is at least as old as an 1844 school book, and it takes a few landmarks you know well rather than talent.
