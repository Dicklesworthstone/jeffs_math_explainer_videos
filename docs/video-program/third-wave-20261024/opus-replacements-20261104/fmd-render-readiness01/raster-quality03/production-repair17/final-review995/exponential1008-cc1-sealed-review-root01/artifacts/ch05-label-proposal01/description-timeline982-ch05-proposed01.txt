Draw a curve whose slope always equals its height. Why should that simple rule lead to factorials, compound interest, a calculating ruler, and a walk around a circle?

We build the exponential function from the ground up. A power series, a limit of tiny multiplications, and a differential equation turn out to describe the same object. Its central rule, that adding inputs multiplies outputs, explains both how a slide rule works and why the exponential keeps appearing in models of change.

The examples include a chemical reaction, a charging capacitor, changing growth rates, compound interest, and random multipliers. We examine where the models apply and where their assumptions fail. Then we turn the rate sideways into the complex plane and build toward Euler's identity through three pictures: a circular walk, shrinking multiplication steps, and a spiral of series terms.
