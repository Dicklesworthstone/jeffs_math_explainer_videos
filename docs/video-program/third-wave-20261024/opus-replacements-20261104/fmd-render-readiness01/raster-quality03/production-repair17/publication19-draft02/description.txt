A smooth curve can hide a surprise: its Taylor series stops working even though the curve itself has no visible trouble. To find the obstruction, we leave the real line and explore the complex plane.

Follow grids, circles and colors through polynomials, reciprocals, exponentials and trigonometric functions. See why a complex derivative has to agree from every direction, and why being holomorphic throughout a region is such a strong condition.

We build the proofs as pictures: shared edges cancel, nested squares shrink, and a tiny loop reveals Cauchy's integral formula. From there we count zeros and poles, use a residue to find a real area, and follow analytic continuation beyond a power series' original disk. We also examine where these tools stop working.
