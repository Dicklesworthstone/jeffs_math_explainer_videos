Why do equal steps on a piano keyboard multiply frequency instead of adding it? That question gives us a logarithmic ruler, a working slide rule, and a way to understand what logarithms really measure.

Then we bend the ruler into a circle and meet the natural logarithm, which undoes exponential growth. The circle's forgotten laps become the key to complex logarithms: infinitely many answers, winding numbers, branch cuts, and a product rule that must keep track of whole turns.

Finally, we move from numbers to matrices. A matrix logarithm describes a steady flow that reaches a given transformation. We build examples with many possible flows, prove why some transformations have no real logarithm, and finish with a three-dimensional spinning-cube puzzle.

We build the pictures step by step, with questions to pause and work through along the way. The film starts from doubling and ratios, uses the idea of a slope, and introduces complex numbers and matrices as it reaches them. The later chapters include full arguments, while a few general theorems are stated with their sources rather than proved.
