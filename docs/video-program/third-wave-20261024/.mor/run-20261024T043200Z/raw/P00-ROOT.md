## Chosen five
1. **Why Your Bus Is Always Late — Even When the Timetable Is Right** (`inspection_paradox`, existing Bead .13). Begin with alternating 2- and 10-minute gaps. Equal numbers of short and long gaps do not mean equal passenger experience. Derive residual-wait triangles, compare event sampling and time sampling. Do not confuse this with buses dynamically bunching.
2. **How Much Can Five Serial Numbers Reveal?** (`german_tank`, existing Bead .12). Begin with five numbered products, not an unsupported war anecdote. A maximum is a lower bound, then gaps earn the correction. Exact uniform sampling without replacement model; show a biased-sampling counterexample. Distinguish unbiasedness, uncertainty and one realised estimate.
3. **Three Keys Open the Secret. Two Reveal Nothing.** (`shamir_secret_sharing`, existing Bead .46). Begin with three of five trustees opening a vault, and the question whether two pieces leak anything. Use a small prime field with enumerated equally likely secrets under fewer than threshold shares. Explain modular arithmetic before invoking polynomials. No production-crypto claims.
4. **Measure a Curve Without Following It** (`crofton`, existing Bead .22). Begin with an awkward curved coastline on a card and a transparent ruler grid. Count crossings, rotate directions, derive length with the correct invariant line measure. Show sampling bias and scale; distinguish an exact integral identity from a finite random estimate.
5. **Two Losing Games Can Win — Here Is the Catch** (`parrondo`, existing Bead .15). Begin with exact rules and a tiny three-state probability diagram. Compute stationary weights and drifts for A, B and a specified mixture; visible redistribution is the reveal. No universal switching claim, financial advice or unverified historical flourish.
