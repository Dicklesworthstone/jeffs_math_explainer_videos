## How to Catch a Change Without Crying Wolf

Two frame times stand out in a real terminal UI recording. Is it time to change what the renderer does? Follow every multiplication in an evidence account and see why a test that works at one fixed time can raise too many false alarms when it runs after every frame.

Then follow actual wine chemistry measurements through a controlled change in the stream. We calculate randomized insertion ranks by hand, turn them into a conformal betting process, and show exactly when the alarm fires. Along the way we derive the stopping guarantee and examine what a production controller has to assume to use it.

## How Wide Should an Honest Prediction Be?

Two stations forecast the same temperature for tomorrow. How much uncertainty should each show? Build a split conformal interval from past forecast errors and count the possible holdouts to see where the coverage guarantee comes from. Then use real wine chemistry data to fit an alcohol predictor, calibrate its intervals, and check them on held-out samples.

We compare the intervals with a Student t benchmark, examine how coverage changes across acidity groups, and see what happens when a method calibrated on white wines faces red wines. That last replay makes the assumptions behind weighted conformal prediction visible.

Four incoming jobs, four servers, and two lists of possible destinations. Watch how each placement changes if you look at both servers and choose the shorter queue. Then count every possible tape for the four-job example and follow the math into a running system where jobs arrive and leave.

## The Math Behind FrankenRobotics

An arm has two angles that wrap around like circles. A rover has poses you can multiply, and turning before you drive changes the result. A six-legged robot has a finite symmetry group that permutes its legs. Each model lets you compute something different about the body and its motion.

We build those representations from the pieces you can see, then multiply matrices, calculate wheel speeds, work through a sideways rover maneuver, and follow a robot's joints down a tree to its feet. The same algebra helps explain what changes when a foot is planted, a snake bends through a closed cycle, or a leg reaches its load limit.
