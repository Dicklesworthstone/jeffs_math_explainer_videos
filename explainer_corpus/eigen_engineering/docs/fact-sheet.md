## Findings (uncomfortable first)

1. **FrankenSim does not hold two of the owner's three applications.** He
   wrote "see my ~/projects/frankensim for tons of stuff like this". Read
   only, at commit `e76c94213`: it has the generalized eigenproblem
   K·φ = λ·M·φ as a first-class facility (`crates/fs-modal/src/lib.rs`,
   shift-invert Lanczos with certified mode counts), a dense cyclic-Jacobi
   kernel (`crates/fs-la/src/eigen.rs`), steel frames with natural
   frequencies (`crates/fs-solid/src/frame2d.rs`), the closed-form modes of
   a rectangular cavity (`crates/fs-couple/src/vibroacoustic.rs`,
   `rectangular_cavity_modes`) and an exterior Helmholtz boundary-element
   solver (`crates/fs-bem/src/helmholtz.rs`). It has **no pin-jointed truss
   solved by a stiffness table** (`crates/fs-truss` is a linear program for
   the lightest truss), **no room acoustics** (its own notes say "no room
   acoustics", and "modal-density / statistical-energy regimes" are listed
   as deferred in `vibroacoustic.rs`), and **nothing on input-output
   economics**. So the truss, the rooms and the economy are computed in this
   crate, and FrankenSim is cited where the video's subject is really there:
   lower-thirds (07_6, 09_2, 10_1, 11_5 and 14_5).
2. **The factory problem of his second image is not an eigenproblem.** Its
   optimum (40, 20) and profit 460 are right (`K-L2.best`, checked against
   all 1,361 allowed whole-number plans, `K-X5.plans`). Its plotted corner
   (0, 60) breaks the aluminium limit (`K-L2.not_a_corner_breaks`); the
   corners are (0, 0), (0, 40), (40, 20), (50, 0). It is linear programming,
   and the video says so in chapter 14 rather than dress it as eigenvectors.
3. **A concert hall's sound is not its modes.** A box with Boston Symphony
   Hall's volume has 1,964,040 modes below 1 kHz (`K-H2.count.1000`), about
   5,800 in every hertz there (`K-H2.per_hz_at_1000`), each about 1.2 Hz
   wide (`K-H2.mode_width_hz`), so some 6,700 modes sit inside one mode's
   width; its Schroeder frequency is 20.2 Hz (`K-H3.schroeder_hz_1dp`), the
   bottom of hearing. So "the acoustics of a proposed concert hall from its
   3D model" is, for everything audible, statistics and rays, not an
   eigen-solve. The video tells that (chapters 10 and 11) and puts the modes
   where they do rule: small rooms, and shapes with no formula, cut into
   cells (an attic studio, modes at 29.8, 56.1 and 59.9 Hz, `K-N1.hz.*`).
4. **For one static load nobody eigendecomposes K.** Real programs factor it
   (FrankenSim's frame code solves statics by LU, and keeps Lanczos for
   modes). The split over patterns explains the answer; it is not how the
   answer is computed. A lower-third at 05_7 says so.
5. **One pattern gives the sag and not the forces.** The softest of the
   truss's 21 patterns alone gives 99.6% of the midspan sag
   (`K-P2.sag_percent.1`) while the worst member force is off by 82 kN, 18%
   of the largest force (`K-P2.force_error_kn.1`,
   `K-P2.force_error_percent.1`); with 5 patterns it is
   still 44 kN, with 15, 8 kN. With two patterns the sag overshoots to
   102.0%. Chapter 06 says both.
6. **The sources on the Millennium Bridge do not say "nothing broke" or
   "it was strong enough"**, and the first draft of the brief did. What they
   say: the engineers' paper calls the cause a loading effect "which had not
   been anticipated during design"; vertical pedestrian excitation had been
   assessed during design by a modified BS 5400 approach; Arup's page says
   the British Standard "is being updated to cover the phenomenon". The
   script says exactly that much (01_4) and nothing about strength except as
   the question (01_5) and 08_7's "Neither bridge's trouble was strength".
7. **Why the sway grew is disputed, and the script stays true under both
   accounts.** Dallard et al. (2001) and Strogatz et al. (2005) have walkers
   falling into step with the deck; Belykh et al. (2021) find that
   synchrony is "a consequence of, not a cause of the instability" and that
   uncorrelated walkers give negative damping on average. All agree on a
   critical crowd size and on a sideways force that grows with the deck's
   motion (Dallard: "As the amplitude of the motion increases, the lateral
   force imparted by individuals increases"). 08_5 says only that. Neither
   "synchrony" nor "fell into step" is spoken. The same paper says the
   design model was right: "The measured frequencies of the modes which had
   been observed to vibrate were within 10% of the values predicted in the
   design analysis … the movements were due to an unpredicted external
   force, rather than unpredicted or miscalculated bridge dynamic
   characteristics." The first ending ("its engineers had learned a second
   question") contradicted that; review R1 caught it, and 14_6 and 14_7 now
   say what the source says.
8. **Tacoma: 12 a minute at the end, 14 at the onset, and "not resonance"
   needs its qualifier.** The board's report has the torsional motion
   starting at 14 cycles a minute; Farquharson's figure for the final motion,
   which Billah and Scanlan use, is 12 (0.2 Hz). Billah and Scanlan rule out
   resonance with vortex shedding (which at 42 mph would come "close to
   1 Hz") and call it single-degree-of-freedom torsional flutter, a
   self-excitation; they add that one may still call it resonance "if we now
   identify the source of the periodic impulses as self-induced". 08_2 names
   the thing the textbooks got wrong, "the wind puffing in time with the
   bridge", and does not say "it was not resonance" bare. Billah and
   Scanlan's page quotations were read by the research pass in the scan at
   ketchum.org; the copy kept outside the repository holds only the
   abstract, so at R1 they were not re-read (the board's report and the
   abstract were). At R2 the scan's page 3 (journal p. 120) was re-read and
   is quoted in S7 below.
9. **Sabine did not design Symphony Hall, and 0.161 is not his number.** He
   advised its architects (his 1900 paper takes "the new Boston Music Hall"
   as its example); his own metric constant was 0.164. The card says
   "Sabine advised the architects of Boston Symphony Hall, which opened in
   1900" (R2 R-09 removed "worked out this way before it was built", which no
   source row records); the screen shows 0.161 as the modern constant.
10. **"Eigen" before eigenvalues: the same word, and no more than that.**
    Helmholtz writes of the *Eigentöne* of a body of air in 1863 (and of
    "eigene Töne" of an enclosed mass of air in 1860); Hilbert introduces
    *Eigenfunktionen* and *Eigenwerte* in 1904, for integral equations, and
    his Eigenwerte are the reciprocals of today's eigenvalues. Whether
    Hilbert had Helmholtz's word in mind is conjecture (Aldrich). The card
    in 09_5's listen window states the two dates and the gap.
11. **The model hall is a box with Boston's volume, not Boston's hall.**
    45 by 23 by 18 m is 18,630 m³ against Beranek's 18,750 (99.4%,
    `K-H1.volume_percent`). Beranek's own width, height and length (22.9,
    18.6 and 39 m) are averages, his length runs from the stage front, and
    their product is not the volume. The screen tags the box "a model".
12. **E = 200 GPa is the US round figure** (29,000 ksi); Eurocode practice
    uses 210. The truss, its sections and its loads are a model.
13. **"Cannot keep up" is exact here and needs care in general.** For a
    table with no negative entry, every demand can be met exactly when the
    largest eigenvalue is below one (equivalently the Hawkins–Simon
    condition). When it is one or more, an economy whose table has no zero
    entry can meet no demand at all; a table that splits into separate parts
    may still meet some. The lower-third of 13_7 carries the condition, and
    the spoken line (after the P3 claim walk) says "some demand cannot".
14. **His first image's seven-zone energy model is not used as a
    calculation.** It is a toy with no source for its numbers. Its true
    cousin, Sabine's statistics, carries 11_1 instead.
15. **The first palette failed the contrast test**: amber sat 14.9 from
    gold, pink 16.7 from the quiz violet, teal 17.3 from green (ΔE2000). The
    out-arrow is now orange `#F2994A`, squeezed magenta `#E0408F`, stretched
    deep teal `#26867C`.
