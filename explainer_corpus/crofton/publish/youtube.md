```text
Put a transparent sheet of parallel lines over a coast and count the crossings. A single placement gives an unreliable length estimate. Averaging over uniformly chosen slides and turns makes the mean crossing count proportional to the curve's length.

Coins, straight sticks and bent wires show how crossings relate to projected length. We use these examples to derive the Cauchy-Crofton formula, examine curves of constant width, and compare different ways to choose a random line. Computed experiments measure the uncertainty left by a finite sample.

The exact identity applies to finite-length plane paths with every traversal counted. Finite samples still have error, and a coastline measured at changing resolution raises a different question.
```
