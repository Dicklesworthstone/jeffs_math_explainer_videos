```text
Put a transparent sheet of parallel lines over a coast and count the crossings. One placement gives the wrong length. But average over the right slides and turns, and the count reveals the length of the curve.

The same coast, a coin, a straight stick and a bent wire carry the argument from shadows to the Cauchy-Crofton formula. Along the way we meet curves of constant width, see why different recipes for a random line give different answers, and measure the uncertainty of finite experiments.

The exact identity applies to finite-length plane paths with every traversal counted. Finite samples still have error, and a coastline measured at changing resolution raises a different question.
```
