```text
A vault has five trustees. Any three should be able to recover its secret. Any two should learn nothing about it. How can a few numbers do both?

An eleven-mark dial lets us build the scheme from scratch. We count every possible dealing, watch ordinary arithmetic leak information, discover why the dial needs a prime number of marks, and reconstruct the secret with helper curves. Then we test what happens when one trustee hands over a false share.

The privacy argument assumes an honest dealer and fair, independent secret random choices. The small dial is a teaching example; the arithmetic alone does not authenticate shares or erase what someone already knew.
```
