```text
Five trustees receive one share each. Any three should be able to recover the secret. Any two should learn nothing about it. How can a few numbers satisfy both conditions?

We build Shamir's scheme on an eleven-mark dial, count every possible dealing, and compare it with ordinary arithmetic that leaks information. Prime-sized dials make the reconstruction work for any three trustees. Helper curves show how to calculate the secret, and a false share tests what the scheme can detect.

The privacy argument assumes an honest dealer whose secret random choices are fair and independent of each other and the secret. The small dial is a teaching example. The arithmetic does not authenticate shares or erase information someone already had.
```
