# Reproducing the adaptive-grid experiment

The 49:42 chapter of [How to Teach a Computer to Simulate a Fluid](../../../narration_lab/third-wave-20261024/root/five-film-release-20261027/navier-production-20261120/owner-review08/navier_stokes_simulation_delivery_master.mp4) compares two grids on a flow with a known answer. The fluid is periodic in a unit square. Its vertical speed is 0.35, so the horizontal shear layer drifts upward. Viscosity spreads the layer. We know exactly how fast each Fourier mode decays. [Native solver source](../../../tools/fluid_reference/periodic_shear.c)

Writing the horizontal velocity as \(U(y,t)\) gives

\[
\partial_t U + 0.35\,\partial_y U = 0.001\,\partial_y^2 U.
\]

The solver starts with a horizontal drift of 0.3 plus 18 Fourier modes. At time \(t\), a mode of wavenumber \(k=2\pi m\) moves with the layer and its amplitude is multiplied by \(\exp(-0.001 k^2t)\). This supplies the reference velocity at each grid point.

## Check pressure and time separately

For a square cell of width \(h\), the solver measures the flux left over after the pressure correction by computing

\[
\frac{\Delta t}{h}\left|(u^x_{\mathrm{right}}-u^x_{\mathrm{left}})+(u^y_{\mathrm{top}}-u^y_{\mathrm{bottom}})\right|.
\]

The four \(u\)'s are the velocity components normal to their faces, in the positive coordinate directions. The expression is the unbalanced volume over one step divided by cell volume, using unit depth. We measure it after the pressure correction and before any mesh change at that step. This exact periodic flow has constant pressure. The adaptive run at a budget of 10⁻⁹ reaches a maximum imbalance of 6.50 × 10⁻¹², already below the second budget of 10⁻¹¹. The tighter run reaches 7.32 × 10⁻¹⁴, and the largest sampled velocity-error fraction changes by 6.18 × 10⁻¹⁰. This tests the sampled error and the measured imbalance for this flow. [Step calculation](../../../tools/fluid_reference/periodic_shear.c) · [Measured runs](../../../narration_lab/third-wave-20261024/root/five-film-release-20261027/navier-solver-shear03/comparison.json)

The solver asks whether the mesh should change every four steps. With half the timestep cap, there are 342 steps and 27 mesh changes. The peak whole-flow error rises from 0.129% to 0.141%. Comparing that run with the 185-step run measures the combined effect of timestep and mesh scheduling. ## Where fine cells matter around a cylinder

We took 12 saved velocity fields on the 2.5 mm grid and grouped each set of four fully fluid cells into a possible 5 mm cell. The mean velocity preserves their total momentum at constant density. The variation around that mean measures how much detail the coarser cell would lose. We kept fine cells near the cylinder wherever a block includes any solid.

We ranked eligible blocks on the first frame in each case and kept the top quarter fine at the next five saved times. The partition uses 63,932 cell slots instead of 144,320 before enforcing quadtree balance. Both counts include 1,176 solid cells. Relative to the whole-flow velocity, the representation error is 0.708–0.709% on the late Re = 20 fields and 0.934–1.028% in the moving Re = 100 wake. Re-selecting the same number of blocks on each saved wake frame gives 0.880–0.882%. The fixed selection and the per-frame selection share 56.7–80.4% of their blocks.
